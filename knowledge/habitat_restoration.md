# Habitat Fragmentation, Ecological Corridors, and Rewilding

## Overview
Fragmentation divides continuous natural ecosystems into insular remnants surrounded by hostile anthropogenic matrices (intensive cropland, roads, urban sprawl). Island biogeography models demonstrate that small, isolated fragments exhibit diminished carrying capacity, amplified demographic stochasticity, inbreeding depression, and elevated extinction debt.

## Landscape Spatial Metrics
- **Connectivity Index (Gamma & Alpha Indices)**: Evaluates the proportion of operational ecological links relative to maximum potential network pathways.
- **Structural vs. Functional Connectivity**: Structural connectivity refers to physical spatial contiguity (e.g., unbroken tree line); functional connectivity describes whether target wildlife actually traverse the matrix.
- **Core Area Ratio**: Proportion of habitat patch isolated from perimeter edge degradation (>100m from borders).

## Restoration Interventions
1. **Linear and Stepping-Stone Corridors**: Establishing 20–50m wide native tree and shrub strips connecting isolated forest fragments lowers dispersal mortality and elevates gene exchange (UNEP, 2021).
2. **Riparian Buffer Corridors**: Vegetating riverbanks and stream orders with native willow, poplar, and reeds creates dual-benefit networks that filter agrochemical run-off while facilitating wildlife transit.
3. **Assisted Natural Regeneration (ANR)**: Removing livestock grazing pressure and invasive weed barriers to allow pioneer native seeds to regenerate spontaneously, achieving restoration at 70% lower cost than manual seedling plantations.
